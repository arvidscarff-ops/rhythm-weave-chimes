**Document role:** Visual and experiential bible  
**Run:** 6 of the PHASE Project Bible documentation plan  
**Authority:** Governing art-direction principles and anti-references  
**Evidence boundary:** Several referenced/generated images are unavailable in the retrieved conversation; this document does not reconstruct image-dependent final designs  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-02

## 1. Visual thesis

> **PHASE is an ancient future seen in cold daylight: sacred utility, industrial mysticism, living weather, mathematical instrumentation, and breathtaking transformations that are beautiful enough to approach and wrong enough to fear.**

The world should look as though:

- humanity possesses technology capable of interfacing with altered reality;
- that technology has been used, repaired, inherited, and ritualized for generations;
- the apocalypse did not erase taste, craft, institutions, or care;
- natural and built matter are converging toward unfamiliar forms;
- flight is necessary work, not military spectacle;
- the sky is both the safest remaining connection and the largest source of uncertainty.

The visual identity is not “post-apocalyptic sci-fi” in general. It is the collision of:

- monumental restraint;
- cold atmospheric life;
- worn institutional continuity;
- precise harmonic mathematics;
- rugged human equipment;
- optimistic visual residues;
- visible, beautiful horror.

## 3. The visual identity in one frame

An ideal PHASE frame might show:

- a quiet glider cockpit at extreme altitude;
- a cold white-blue sky opening over a deep cloud field;
- dark, repaired instrumentation carrying old MTC markings;
- geometric harmonic forms moving with exact musical purpose;
- small signs of human use—wear, tape, handwriting, a token, a replaced panel;
- distant weather that appears serene before its scale or behavior becomes disturbing;
- one saturated note of green, red, or signal color used with discipline;
- no need for darkness to create fear.

The frame should communicate:

> This is beautiful.  
> This is working equipment.  
> This has existed longer than its current user.  
> The sky is behaving according to rules I cannot fully read.

## 4. Governing visual principles

### 4.1 Monumental restraint

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

Scale should be communicated through:

- negative space;
- long horizons;
- slow movement;
- sparse landmarks;
- tiny human/craft references;
- cloud layers;
- restrained framing;
- patient reveals.

Avoid filling every frame with detail to prove scale.

### 4.2 Cold, living atmosphere

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

The world is not a dead grey wasteland.

Its primary visual climate includes:

- green;
- blue;
- cold white;
- blue-grey;
- fog;
- mist;
- cloud;
- water;
- moss;
- oxidized surfaces;
- filtered daylight;
- high-altitude clarity;
- overcast softness.

The palette should feel breathable and alive even when dangerous.

### 4.3 Ancient future

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

Advanced technology has accumulated:

- repairs;
- ritual;
- provenance;
- legacy standards;
- old typography;
- engravings;
- replacement panels;
- inherited markings;
- multiple manufacturing eras;
- craft knowledge.

Do not represent age only through grime. Age appears through continuity.

### 4.4 Sacred utility

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

Objects feel sacred because civilization depends on them and generations have cared for them.

Sacredness should emerge from:

- precision;
- repetition;
- stewardship;
- material durability;
- ceremonial handling;
- visible maintenance;
- encoded knowledge;
- scarcity of replacement;
- social respect.

It should not require gold ornament, robes, altars, or fantasy iconography.

### 4.5 Industrial mysticism

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

The visual language may make engineering feel mystical without making it arbitrary.

Examples:

- harmonic diagrams engraved into structural parts;
- calibration patterns functioning as seals;
- a geometric field that is both instrument and symbol;
- technical clothing whose repeated straps, cavities, and panels have ritual meaning;
- weather displays that resemble liturgical tables because generations rely on them.

Mysticism must remain tethered to function.

### 4.6 Scientific spirituality

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

PHASE should evoke awe through:

- real relationships;
- pattern;
- scale;
- resonance;
- symmetry and controlled asymmetry;
- physical response;
- careful observation.

Avoid decorative “sacred geometry” that has no relationship to the project’s actual musical mathematics.

### 4.7 Rugged humanity

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

The world is maintained by people.

Surfaces and garments should show:

- repair;
- fit adjustment;
- personal markings;
- weather protection;
- repeated handling;
- local improvisation;
- inherited components;
- fatigue;
- pride.

PHASE must not become sterile luxury sci-fi or untouchable ceremony.

### 4.8 Beauty as danger

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

Anomaly design should attract before it repels.

The player should see:

- perfect color;
- seductive motion;
- impossible order;
- luminous depth;
- gentle scale;
- coherent transformation.

Then discover that proximity, duration, repetition, or interaction makes it unsafe.

## 5. Reference-to-principle map

References guide principles, not assets.

### Lucid Rhythms Trigger Engine corpus

**Use:**

- mathematical rhythm made immediately visible;
- multiple voices readable as one composition;
- patient repetition and large-cycle anticipation;
- distinct spatial organizations for distinct musical ideas;
- visual restraint compatible with long listening;
- audiovisual work that can support relaxation, meditation, concentration, and deep work;
- a simple enough initial encounter that the composition itself is the gateway.

**Transform:**

- standalone abstract compositions become PHASE’s entry experience and later acquire world context;
- reference geometry becomes independently designed Trigger Engine families governed by PHASE’s authoritative event model;
- generic presentation becomes PHASE’s own cold, living, ancient-future visual language;
- reference-level long-form appeal becomes an interactive, reconstructable, accessible runtime.

**Do not copy:**

- exact compositions;
- audio;
- visual assets;
- exact layouts, colors, effects, titles, thumbnails, or branding;
- protected audiovisual expression;
- inferred private implementation details presented as fact.

- ordinary places becoming perceptually wrong;

**Use:**

- discipline;
- repeated practice;
- restrained silhouette;
- collective visual code;
- objects worn through ritual use.

**Do not copy:**

- fantasy priesthood;
- ceremonial robes unsuitable for work;
- religious symbols without in-world history.

## 6. Color system

### 6.1 World palette

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

Primary tendencies:

| Family | Role |
|---|---|
| Cold white | cloud, altitude, clarity, institutional space, exposure |
| Blue-grey | atmosphere, distance, metal, weather, interface foundation |
| Deep blue | depth, night, instrumentation, shadow without pure black |
| Moss/lichen green | living persistence, altered ecology, old surfaces |
| Oxidized green/blue | age, mineral/weather exposure, maintained infrastructure |
| Charcoal/black | gliders, working gear, contrast, monolithic equipment |
| Pale mineral neutrals | architecture, uniforms, paper, aged polymers |

### 6.2 Accent colors

Use sparingly and semantically.

Possible roles:

- warning;
- route state;
- harmonic voice;
- institutional division;
- anomaly class;
- interaction focus.

Exploratory red/white/green progression families are not canon.

### 6.3 Saturation

Most of the world should remain restrained enough that:

- a saturated anomaly feels significant;
- one red signal matters;
- luminous green can feel holy or toxic;
- warm human light feels intimate;
- dawn/sunset does not become visual wallpaper.

### 6.4 Black and white

MTC marks, diagrams, patches, stamps, and manuals must work in pure black/white vector form where appropriate.

No identity should depend entirely on:

- gradient;
- glow;
- transparency;
- 3D material;
- animation.

### 6.5 Gradients

Gradients are welcome in atmosphere, light, cloud, and rendered harmonic fields.

They should not be required for:

- core icon recognition;
- safety state;
- typography;
- institutional seals;
- small-scale reproduction.

## 7. Light and daylight horror

### 7.1 Daylight is primary

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

PHASE’s horror must function in:

- clear morning;
- white overcast;
- high noon;
- blue distance;
- green reflected light;
- sunrise;
- visible cloud fields.

### 7.2 Light behavior

Prefer:

- slow volumetric change;
- deep atmospheric perspective;
- soft but directional daylight;
- glare shaped by altitude;
- cloud shadow;
- luminous particulate depth;
- subtle spectral change near anomalies.

Avoid:

- constant underexposure;
- crushed blacks;
- horror-vignette clichés;
- strobing as tension;
- red emergency light in every dangerous scene.

### 7.3 Wrongness in full view

Visual horror can emerge when:

- cloud layers align too perfectly;
- a distant object does not change size correctly;
- shadows move with another rhythm;
- vegetation repeats mathematically;
- a beautiful gradient remains fixed while the camera moves;
- an object’s scale becomes evident only through a glider passing near it;
- symmetry is locally perfect but globally impossible.

These are examples, not canonical anomalies.

## 8. Material language

### 8.1 MTC and glider materials

**STRONG DIRECTION**

- dark composites;
- coated metals;
- matte technical ceramics;
- cold glass;
- translucent instrument layers;
- woven high-performance textiles;
- oxidized structural metals;
- engraved brass or bronze in inherited objects;
- enamel markings;
- paper/manual stock;
- aged polymers;
- repair tape and field patches used with discipline.

### 8.2 Material contrast

The world should combine:

- monolithic surface ↔ hand repair;
- high precision ↔ accumulated wear;
- advanced composite ↔ old brass token;
- translucent field ↔ opaque equipment;
- clean cloud light ↔ dark working cockpit;
- living moss ↔ structural metal.

### 8.3 Wear

Wear should explain use:

- hand contact;
- airflow;
- repeated opening;
- sunlight;
- condensation;
- salt;
- abrasion;
- repair;
- replacement.

Avoid uniform dirt overlays.

### 8.4 Anomalous materials

Anomalous matter should not default to flesh.

Possible qualities:

- impossibly smooth;
- self-similar;
- translucent without visible volume;
- patterned by standing waves;
- mineral-organic ambiguity;
- too symmetrical;
- responsive to sound;
- apparently grown around human structures.

## 9. Shape language

### 9.1 Global contrast

PHASE’s shape language should balance:

- long aerodynamic planes;
- rounded pressure/resonance volumes;
- exact nodal geometry;
- tall or vertical stabilizing forms;
- worn rectangular institutional hardware;
- soft environmental mass;
- sparse fine technical lines.

### 9.2 Human systems

Human/MTC shapes should feel:

- designed;
- maintainable;
- modular;
- comprehensible;
- marked by standards;
- able to survive multiple eras of repair.

### 9.3 Anomalous systems

Anomaly shapes may feel:

- discovered rather than designed;
- mathematically coherent;
- organic and mineral simultaneously;
- indifferent to human scale;
- beautiful at a glance and impossible under inspection.

### 9.4 Ornament

Ornament is acceptable when it is:

- generated by working mathematics;
- inherited from an institution;
- added by crews;
- encoding identity, history, calibration, or protection;
- visibly produced through craft.

Avoid arbitrary filigree used only to say “ancient.”

## 10. Harmonic mathematics as visual grammar

### 10.1 Core rule

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

Mathematics should generate form, not merely annotate it.

Do not draw an arbitrary logo and place a formula beside it.

### 10.2 Candidate mathematical families

| Family | Strong visual use | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Chladni modes | nodal fields, seals, structural/harmonic marks | **STRONG DIRECTION** |
| Standing-wave interference | transit, corridor, alignment, safe passage | **STRONG DIRECTION** |
| Lissajous curves | navigation, phase relationships, motion paths | **STRONG DIRECTION** |
| Fourier harmonics | audio, spectral identity, waveform-derived ornament | **STRONG DIRECTION** |
| Polar harmonics | organic/institutional motifs | **EXPLORATORY** |
| Spherical harmonics | cosmology, fields, anomalies, three-dimensional forms | **STRONG DIRECTION** |
| Reaction-diffusion | altered biology/material growth | **EXPLORATORY** |
| Minimal surfaces | membranes, field geometry, glider/anomaly forms | **EXPLORATORY** |

### 10.3 Correctness

When a visual claims to represent:

- a ratio;
- mode;
- standing wave;
- spectral decomposition;
- rhythmic relationship;

the underlying construction should be mathematically valid or clearly labeled as inspired abstraction.

### 10.4 Visual hierarchy

Not every surface should carry harmonic geometry.

Use it where the world would need:

- identity;
- calibration;
- classification;
- orientation;
- protection;
- instruction;
- ceremonial recognition.

Overuse turns the strongest idea into wallpaper.

### 10.5 Motion

Harmonic motion should derive from authoritative musical state.

Visual elegance cannot override the timing rules in `AUDIO_RHYTHM_ARCHITECTURE.md`.

## 11. MTC identity system

### 11.1 Identity posture

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

MTC should feel like:

- an old scientific institution;
- a transit utility;
- a professional order;
- a maintenance culture;
- a civilizational standard.

It should not feel like a contemporary startup identity exercise.

### 11.2 Identity surfaces

Design the system across:

- aircraft markings;
- uniforms;
- route maps;
- seals;
- maintenance stamps;
- certification marks;
- instrument labels;
- manuals;
- brass/bronze tokens;
- weather stations;
- hangars;
- cargo/data containers;
- signage;
- patches;
- archival documents;
- digital UI.

The logo should emerge from the system, not carry all meaning alone.

### 11.3 Logo constraints

The final mark should:

- work in black and white;
- survive small sizes;
- work engraved, stamped, painted, stitched, and displayed;
- feel plausible on a glider wing;
- connect to harmony/alignment through form;
- avoid generic airline/navigation imagery;
- possess enough simplicity to accumulate cultural weight.

### 11.4 Rejected logo language

Do not default to:

- wings;
- compass;
- globe;
- anchor;
- nautical crest;
- airline swoosh;
- shipping badge;
- generic star/twinkle;
- royal coat of arms;
- ornamental blob with decorative formula.

### 11.5 Image-dependent decision warning

**UNRESOLVED**

The conversation records:

- selection of “#3” from a logo set;
- preference for “Resonance Orbit” and “Node Alignment” directions;
- requests for black/white vector refinement;
- selection among image variants.

The actual images are not available in the retrieved context.

Therefore:

- no exact MTC mark is canonized here;

### 11.6 Flying squirrel motif

It may function as:

- crew art;
- a historical MTC motif;
- patch;
- secondary mark;
- folk symbol.

It is not safely established as the final primary MTC logo.

### 11.7 Other natural motifs

Exploratory:

- maple samara;
- albatross;
- swift;
- manta ray;
- moth;
- dandelion seed.

Do not present these as canon.

## 12. Typography

### 12.1 Institutional typography

**STRONG DIRECTION**

Typography should feel:

- legible;
- durable;
- standardized;
- multilingual;
- technical;
- able to exist across many manufacturing eras.

### 12.2 Historical layering

MTC may use:

- a core institutional face;
- older legacy labels;
- monospaced technical notation;
- engraved numerals;
- stencil/maintenance lettering;
- handwritten annotations;
- local destination typography.

One perfectly consistent modern brand font across every century would feel artificial.

### 12.3 Avoid

- generic sci-fi wide tracking;
- unreadable techno fonts;
- excessive all-caps everywhere;
- faux-Latin luxury serif as sacred shorthand;
- startup grotesk as the only layer;
- random typographic variation with no provenance.

### 12.4 Naming language

Names should be pronounceable and institutionally credible, with Scandinavian/European/Latin tendencies. Typography should support Swedish, English, German, and future invented terms without exoticizing them.

## 13. Navigator and Meridian attire

### 13.1 Core attire principle

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

The wearer must look capable of doing difficult work in a late-stage altered world.

Near-sacred status is layered onto workwear; it does not replace it.

### 13.2 Flight attire

**STRONG DIRECTION**

Possible components:

- pressure/weather layers;
- harness interfaces;
- calibration points;
- hearing/respiration protection;
- modular pockets;
- tactile controls;
- reinforced contact areas;
- replaceable panels;
- personal/route markings;
- MTC certification;
- inherited or repaired pieces;
- restrained harmonic patterning.

Every visible element should have a plausible work, safety, sensory, institutional, or ritual function.

### 13.3 Public attire

**STRONG DIRECTION**

When seen away from flight:

- the Navigator should remain recognizable through posture, cut, material, or retained equipment;
- clothing may be more formal without becoming court costume;
- age and service should remain visible;
- institutional respect may be expressed through restraint rather than ornament.

### 13.4 Meridian distinction

**UNRESOLVED**

Because Meridian’s meaning is unresolved, no canonical Meridian uniform exists.

If a visual distinction is later adopted, it should avoid:

- crowns;
- glowing robes;
- fantasy priest silhouettes;
- arbitrary gold;
- superhero capes;
- unwearable ceremony.

Potential distinction may emerge from:

- accumulated physiological change;
- exceptional simplicity;
- older/custom equipment;
- absence of ordinary rank markers;
- material or geometric calibration unique to their state.

### 13.5 Working mystic

Desired impression:

> A person whose rituals are real procedures, whose clothing has survived actual work, and whose near-religious presence comes from mastery rather than costume.

## 14. Glider design language

### 14.1 Core identity

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

Gliders embody:

- lift;
- efficiency;
- silence;
- long endurance;
- atmospheric reading;
- civilizational necessity;
- maintenance;
- institutional history.

### 14.2 Silhouette

**STRONG DIRECTION**

Promising qualities:

- high aspect ratio;
- blended or tailless volumes;
- sail-like planes;
- vertical harmonic/stabilizing structures;
- a recognizable silhouette at distance;
- no visual dependence on weapons or giant engines.

These are directions, not a finalized airframe.

### 14.3 Visual equation

Exploratory shorthand:

> **sailplane × stealth precision × cathedral instrument × working cargo vehicle**

Use the principle, not literal collage.

### 14.4 Surface treatment

Gliders may combine:

- large calm monolithic surfaces;
- small dense maintenance zones;
- precise technical markings;
- crew-applied decals/sigils;
- route history;
- repair patches;
- panel replacement across eras;
- restrained reflective or harmonic surfaces.

### 14.5 Propulsion

Visually favor quiet flight and unobtrusive propulsion.

Do not canonically remove all propulsion without a lore/engineering decision.

### 14.6 Families

Courier, transit, cargo, high-altitude, and ancient airframes are **EXPLORATORY**.

### 14.7 Anti-reference

Avoid:

- fighter jet nose/aggression;
- missile pylons;
- military stealth copy;
- generic spaceship fuselage;
- exposed neon engines;
- random greebling;
- implausibly delicate ornament;
- luxury yacht language;
- one-piece pristine concept-car surfaces without maintenance access.

### 14.8 Image-dependent warning

Three glider references and proposed concept sheets are mentioned but not included. No final glider design can be derived from text alone.

## 15. Cockpit and transit point of view

### 15.1 Cockpit role

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

The cockpit is the bridge between:

- world;
- instrument;
- Navigator;
- route;
- composition.

### 15.2 View

The sky/environment must retain priority.

Instrumentation should frame and interpret the view rather than cover it.

### 15.3 Layering

Potential layers:

1. physical cockpit/material frame;
2. embedded instruments;
3. harmonic/Trigger Engine geometry;
4. route and forecast state;
5. transmissions;
6. exterior environment;
7. anomaly response.

These must be hierarchically composed.

### 15.4 Physical and projected UI

The interface may combine:

- physical controls;
- embedded screens;
- transparent projection;
- field visualization;
- non-diegetic usability layer.

Pure diegesis is not required if it damages legibility.

### 15.5 Personal history

The cockpit may show:

- worn contact points;
- notes;
- tokens;
- repair history;
- route marks;
- crew/Navigator customization.

Avoid clutter without narrative or functional purpose.

### 15.6 Camera

For long sessions:

- motion must be slow and comfortable;
- horizon behavior must avoid nausea;
- turbulence must be adjustable;
- camera effects must not constantly perform;
- reduced-motion options must preserve route information.

## 16. Skies, clouds, and atmosphere

### 16.1 Sky as primary environment

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

The sky is not background content. It is the principal traversed world.

### 16.2 Required range

The system should eventually support:

- clear high-altitude light;
- layered cloud seas;
- transit within cloud;
- fog and low visibility;
- rain;
- storm;
- dawn;
- day;
- sunset;
- night;
- distant terrain/ocean;
- altered atmospheric phenomena.

This is a target range, not prototype scope.

### 16.3 Movement

Forward movement should feel:

- steady;
- relatively slow;
- scale-aware;
- continuous;
- capable of sustaining 10–90 minute experiences.

Speed should be communicated through parallax, cloud layers, particulate motion, light, and instrument state—not only camera shake.

### 16.4 Procedural/hybrid direction

**STRONG DIRECTION**

One adaptable sky engine is preferable to route-specific long videos.

The world/weather simulation should provide parameters to the environment.

### 16.5 Visual repetition

Use:

- multi-scale noise;
- evolving cloud topology;
- time-of-day change;
- weather;
- route-specific altitude/biome;
- slow rare events;
- authored transitions.

Avoid obvious short loops.

### 16.6 Performance

The visual ambition must be tested in the target runtime before production scale is committed.

Beautiful cloud rendering that cannot remain stable during long web sessions does not satisfy the product.

## 17. Weather visual language

### 17.1 Weather is systemic

Weather must visibly agree with:

- forecast;
- route state;
- sound;
- duration;
- danger;
- instrumentation.

### 17.2 Ordinary weather

Do not make every condition anomalous.

Ordinary:

- cloud;
- rain;
- wind;
- clear air;
- overcast;
- changing light;

must be beautiful enough to sustain the product.

### 17.3 Anomalous weather

When introduced, it should modify:

- movement logic;
- color relationships;
- repetition;
- geometry;
- material response;
- sound-visible coupling;

not merely add a colored filter.

### 17.4 Forecast graphics

Forecast UI may combine:

- familiar meteorological legibility;
- route graph;
- uncertainty;
- harmonic indicators;
- MTC standards;
- historical comparison.

It should feel used daily, not like an occult prophecy screen.

### 17.5 Warnings

Warnings should be:

- calm;
- categorical;
- legible;
- restrained;
- serious because they are rare.

Avoid constant red alert design.

## 18. Zones and altered environments

### 18.1 Visual rule

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

Zones transform; they do not merely decay.

### 18.2 Convergence cues

Possible cues:

- repeated geometry;
- material smoothing;
- structures becoming grown;
- biological/mineral ambiguity;
- standing-wave patterning;
- changes in scale;
- impossible alignment;
- light behaving with local rules;
- sound implied through visual response.

### 18.3 Infestation language

The conversation used “infested,” but the visual system should avoid a single infection metaphor.

A deeply affected place can appear:

- ordered;
- fertile;
- empty;
- crystalline;
- soft;
- overgrown;
- perfectly maintained by no visible agent;
- hostile without dirt or damage.

### 18.4 Boundary design

**UNRESOLVED**

Zone borders are not canonically:

- walls;
- shimmer curtains;
- storm fronts;
- map circles.

Avoid copying familiar anomaly-zone boundaries until lore selects behavior.

### 18.5 Daylight proof

Every core Zone concept should be tested in full daylight.

If it is frightening only when hidden, it does not meet PHASE’s primary horror bar.

## 19. Anomalies and organisms

### 19.1 Anomaly design

An anomaly should have:

- one coherent visual rule;
- a way to observe change over time;
- a scale relationship;
- environmental effect;
- operational consequence;
- restraint.

### 19.2 Organism design

Organisms should appear adapted to transformed conditions rather than designed as game enemies.

Use:

- locomotion shaped by atmosphere;
- senses implied through form;
- harmonic/field response;
- ecology;
- nonhuman priorities.

Avoid:

- glowing weak points;
- combat silhouettes;
- extra limbs as shorthand;
- xenomorph biomechanical copying;
- gore-first anatomy.

### 19.3 Scale

Reveal scale through:

- delayed parallax;
- cloud occlusion;
- glider comparison;
- weather interaction;
- shadow;
- partial visibility.

Do not rely only on a wide shot plus a tiny craft.

### 19.4 Beauty

An organism may be visually graceful, peaceful, or luminous and still be unsafe.

## 20. Architecture and destinations

### 20.1 Current status

**UNRESOLVED**

No canonical destination architecture exists.

### 20.2 Principles

Destinations should express:

- local environment;
- dependence on transit;
- MTC infrastructure;
- adaptation over 130 years;
- repair;
- community;
- inherited pre-arrival layers;
- new cultural form.

### 20.3 Avoid universal ruin

Not every place should be:

- abandoned;
- broken;
- militarized;
- underground;
- industrial.

The world must contain functioning, cared-for spaces.

### 20.4 MTC architecture

Possible qualities:

- legible circulation;
- weather protection;
- hangar scale;
- route-facing observation;
- calibration spaces;
- old standards layered with new systems;
- public utility dignity.

### 20.5 Monumentality

Monumental does not mean enormous everywhere.

A small weather station can feel monumental through isolation, purpose, and horizon.

## 21. Interface and HUD

### 21.1 Interface thesis

The UI should feel like instrumentation belonging to a mature practice.

### 21.2 Priorities

- legibility;
- calm hierarchy;
- low interruption;
- persistent important state;
- long-session comfort;
- musical/visual coherence;
- usable re-entry after attention shifts.

### 21.3 Core transit information

Potentially:

- route;
- destination;
- progress;
- expected/effective duration;
- weather;
- stability;
- composition/voice state;
- transmissions;
- warnings.

The exact set remains a game-system decision.

### 21.4 Visual rhythm

UI motion should:

- derive from authoritative phase where musically meaningful;
- avoid decorative unsynchronized pulsing;
- use slow transitions;
- reserve rapid motion for true operational need;
- remain usable under reduced motion.

### 21.5 Layer hierarchy

1. safety/required action;
2. route/progress;
3. harmonic/music state;
4. environment;
5. narrative;
6. decorative/contextual detail.

This hierarchy may change by mode but must remain explicit.

### 21.6 Non-diegetic UI

Permitted.

Do not sacrifice:

- readability;
- accessibility;
- localization;
- input clarity;

to make every menu appear projected inside the cockpit.

### 21.7 Anti-reference

Avoid:

- generic cyan holograms;
- tiny unreadable sci-fi text;
- infinite concentric circles;
- random waveform decoration;
- constant scan lines;
- glassmorphism applied everywhere;
- red warning overlays for mild states;
- mobile dashboard cards invading transit view;
- decorative numbers without meaning.

## 22. Trigger Engine visual philosophy

### 22.1 Role

Trigger Engines make rhythm spatially and visually perceivable.

### 22.2 Truthfulness

**LOCKED PRINCIPLE**

Geometry must express real musical state.

It cannot simulate accuracy while note timing comes from an unrelated system.

### 22.3 Visual vocabulary

May include:

- nodes;
- orbit;
- alignment;
- paths;
- standing-wave fields;
- pulses;
- phase relationships;
- closure;
- interference;
- density;
- trace/history.

### 22.4 Collision imagery

Objects may visually meet, cross, or activate.

The rendered contact must not authoritatively cause the note.

### 22.5 Complexity

Progression may add visual richness, but:

- early engines must already be beautiful;
- complexity must remain readable;
- more particles do not equal mastery;
- geometry should reveal deeper structure.

### 22.6 Environmental response

Weather/anomaly may modulate:

- material;
- density;
- amplitude;
- color;
- trail;
- field distortion;
- orchestration-linked geometry.

Mappings must be bounded and systemically correct.

## 23. Symbols, seals, patches, and crew markings

### 23.1 Institutional versus personal

Separate:

- MTC master identity;
- division/classification marks;
- route symbols;
- airframe identifiers;
- certification;
- crew/Navigator personal marks;
- inherited flight history.

### 23.2 Crew culture

**STRONG DIRECTION**

Working craft may carry:

- stickers;
- decals;
- route marks;
- names;
- old repairs;
- hand-applied symbols;
- restrained rebellious humor.

Crews can be highly competent without appearing sterile or obedient in every personal detail.

### 23.3 Heraldry

Use carefully.

Heraldic weight may come from:

- repetition across generations;
- symbolic economy;
- recognizable geometry;
- material reproduction;
- provenance.

Avoid royal fantasy crests.

### 23.4 Mathematical classification

Ratios/modes may generate marks for:

- harmonic class;
- route;
- division;
- Trigger Engine;
- anomaly record.

This taxonomy is exploratory until formalized.

## 24. Motion language

### 24.1 Primary motion qualities

- slow;
- continuous;
- phase-aware;
- weighted;
- precise;
- atmospheric;
- capable of long repetition without irritation.

### 24.2 Fast motion

Reserve for:

- real danger;
- control response;
- close environmental event;
- rare anomaly;
- decisive transition.

### 24.3 Easing

Avoid generic UI easing when motion represents physical or musical state.

Use:

- physically motivated interpolation;
- phase-derived motion;
- clear quantized transitions;
- calm UI easing for navigation.

### 24.4 Camera motion

Minimize:

- head bob;
- constant shake;
- dramatic banking for spectacle;
- rapid focus pulls;
- involuntary zoom.

Long-session comfort is part of quality.

---

## 25. Sound-visible relationships

Though audio is defined elsewhere, visual direction must support:

- visible voice identity;
- recognizable phase;
- macro-cycle anticipation;
- exact closure;
- new layers appearing with progression;
- environment affecting both modalities coherently.

Avoid one-to-one clichés such as:

- every sound equals a random particle burst;
- bass equals screen shake;
- danger equals red;
- reverb equals blur.

Mappings should be authored as part of each audiovisual system.

## 26. Accessibility

### 26.1 Reduced motion

Provide:

- stable camera;
- reduced particles;
- reduced rotation;
- reduced flashes;
- persistent phase/state cues;
- equivalent information.

### 26.2 Photosensitivity

Set limits on:

- flash frequency;
- contrast;
- full-screen luminance change;
- rapid geometric alternation.

### 26.3 Color

Never encode safety, route status, or harmonic state through color alone.

Use:

- form;
- label;
- pattern;
- position;
- iconography.

### 26.4 Legibility

Support:

- scalable text;
- contrast modes;
- subtitles/transcripts;
- localization;
- readable cockpit information;
- long viewing comfort.

## 29. Visual review checklist

Before approving a visual:

1. Does it feel cold, living, and atmospheric?
2. Does it support daylight?
3. Is beauty doing more than decorating danger?
4. Does it feel maintained across generations?
5. Is sacredness earned through utility and continuity?
6. Does the object look usable?
7. Does wear have a physical cause?
8. Is mathematical form correct or honestly abstracted?
9. Does it avoid direct reference copying?
10. Does it preserve human scale?
11. Does it remain legible during long use?
12. Does motion derive from meaningful state?
13. Can the identity work without glow/gradient?
14. Does it look like PHASE rather than generic sci-fi?
15. Would it still work beside existing MTC, glider, Zone, and Trigger Engine language?
